### Versioning System

**Protocol Zero uses semantic versioning:**

**Format:**  MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH

**MAJOR version (1. 0, 2.0, 3.0):**  
- Complete restructuring of content
- Fundamental changes to principles or approach
- Breaking changes requiring readers to re-learn structure

**MINOR version (1.1, 1.2, 1.3):**  
- New chapters or sections
- Significant updates to existing content
- New tools or practices added
- Changes that expand scope but don't break existing structure

**PATCH version (1.0.1, 1.0.2, 1.0.3):**  
- Corrections of errors
- Minor clarifications
- Tool link updates
- Small improvements to existing content


### Release Schedule

**Target release cycle:**

**Major versions:**  Every 3-5 years (or as needed when fundamental shifts in technology/privacy landscape occur)

**Minor versions:**  Every 6-12 months (incorporate new tools, practices, threat models)

**Patch versions:**  As needed (immediate corrections for errors, broken links, outdated critical information)

**Emergency updates:**  Immediate release if critical security information becomes outdated or dangerous (e.g., recommended tool suffers major breach, widely-used practice becomes vulnerable)

## What Gets Updated and When

### High Priority (Update Immediately)

**Security vulnerabilities:**
- Recommended tool suffers major breach
- Widely-recommended practice becomes insecure
- New attack vector threatens common defenses

**Legal changes:**
- Major privacy law changes (GDPR amendments, new jurisdictions)
- Changes affecting digital legacy/estate planning

**Obsolete critical tools:**
- Recommended tool discontinued
- Tool significantly changes business model (e.g., becomes hostile to privacy)

**Process:**  Emergency patch release with clear changelog explaining what changed and why. 


### Medium Priority (Update in Next Minor Version)

**New tools and services:**
- Better alternatives to recommended tools emerge
- New categories of tools become relevant

**Evolving threats:**
- New common scam or attack methods
- Changes in platform policies affecting privacy

**Practice improvements:**
- Better methods for accomplishing goals in document
- Simplifications or optimizations

**Technology shifts:**
- Widespread adoption of new technology (e.g., passkeys replacing passwords)
- Obsolescence of old technology (e.g., SMS 2FA being phased out)

**Process:**  Scheduled minor version update with comprehensive changelog.


### Low Priority (Update in Next Major Version or as Accumulated)

**Stylistic improvements:**
- Better examples
- Clearer explanations
- Improved organization within existing structure

**Expanded coverage:**
- New scenarios not previously covered
- Deeper treatment of existing topics
- Additional checklists or templates

**Structural improvements:**
- Reorganization for better flow
- Merging or splitting chapters for clarity

**Process:**  Accumulated and released in batches to avoid constant minor changes.


## Update Process

### 1. Proposal Submission

**Anyone can propose changes through designated channel** (to be established).

**Proposal should include:**
- What needs to change
- Why (what's wrong with current version, or what's missing)
- Proposed new text (if applicable)
- Evidence/sources supporting change
- Impact assessment (minor clarification vs. major shift)


### 2. Review

**Primary Maintainer reviews proposal:**
- Is this accurate?
- Is this consistent with Protocol Zero principles?
- Is this appropriate priority?
- Does this improve the document?

**Subject matter experts consulted** for technical or specialized topics.

**Community feedback solicited** for major changes. 


### 3. Integration

**Approved changes integrated into working draft.**

**Changelog updated** documenting what changed and why.

**Version number incremented** according to significance of change.


### 4. Testing

**Before release:**
- Fact-checking (verify technical accuracy)
- Link checking (all URLs work)
- Consistency checking (new content aligns with existing principles)
- Readability review (maintains accessible language)
- Format checking (markdown renders correctly, templates are usable)


### 5. Release

**Published to primary distribution channel** (website, repository, etc.).

**Announcement** of new version with highlights of major changes.

**Old version archived** but remains accessible (people may link to specific version).

**Translations notified** of changes requiring translation updates.


## Quality Standards

All updates must maintain these core principles:

### 1. Accessibility

**Plain language:**  
No unnecessary jargon.   Technical terms defined clearly.

**Multiple skill levels:**  
Content useful for beginners through advanced users.

**Practical focus:**  
Actionable steps, not just theory.

**Inclusive:**  
Relevant across different: 
- Technical skill levels
- Threat models
- Life situations
- Cultures and jurisdictions (while acknowledging some specifics are regional)


### 2. Accuracy

**Technically correct:**  
Information must be factually accurate.  Expert review for complex topics.

**Sources cited:**  
Claims backed by evidence.   Reputable sources. 

**Current:**  
Information reflects present state, not outdated practices.

**Honest about uncertainty:**  
When practices are debated or situation is evolving, acknowledge this rather than presenting false certainty.


### 3. Impartiality

**Tool recommendations:**  
Based on merit (security, privacy, usability), not sponsorship or affiliation.

**No commercial bias:**  
Document serves readers, not vendors.

**Acknowledge trade-offs:**  
No perfect solutions.   Present options and their pros/cons.

**Platform neutral where possible:**  
Cover Windows, Mac, Linux, iOS, Android.  Acknowledge when advice is platform-specific.


### 4. Respect for Reader

**No fearmongering:**  
Honest about risks without creating paranoia.

**No condescension:**  
Everyone starts as beginner.  Meeting readers where they are.

**No moralizing:**  
"If you care about privacy, consider..." not "you must..."

**Accepting imperfection:**  
Progress over perfection.  Some security is better than none.  Readers make own choices about trade-offs.


### 5. Timelessness When Possible

**Principles over tools:**  
Core chapters focus on enduring principles.   Specific tools in appendix (easier to update).

**Conceptual understanding:**  
Teach *why* something works, not just *what* to click.  When tools change, understanding persists.

**Fundamental practices:**  
Strong passwords, backups, separation of concerns—these won't change even as implementation details do.


## Change Philosophy

### What Should Change

**Technology evolves:**  
Tools, platforms, threats, attacks, defenses all change.  Protocol Zero must reflect current landscape.

**Best practices improve:**  
As community learns, better methods emerge.  Protocol Zero should incorporate improvements.

**Laws and regulations:**  
Privacy laws evolve.   Digital rights expand (or contract).  Protocol Zero adapts.

**Reader feedback:**  
Confusion, errors, missing information reported by readers should be addressed.


### What Should Not Change

**Core principles:**  
- You own your data
- Privacy is not secrecy
- Convenience doesn't require surrender
- Security is practice, not product
- Digital literacy is survival

**Voice and approach:**  
- Plain language
- Practical focus
- Respect for reader
- Inclusive of different situations
- Acceptance of imperfection

**Mission:**  
Protocol Zero remains guide for *everyone* to manage digital life safely, privately, and sustainably—not specialized manual for experts.


## Community Involvement

### Open Contribution Model

**Protocol Zero is community resource.**

**Anyone can:**
- Propose improvements
- Translate to other languages
- Create derivative works (workshops, simplified guides, video explanations)
- Share and distribute freely

**Governance:**  
While contributions are open, final editorial control rests with primary maintainer and contributing editors to ensure consistency and quality.


### Contribution Guidelines

**When proposing changes:**

**Be specific:**  
Not "security chapter needs improvement" but "Step 3 in password manager section assumes Windows; add Mac/Linux instructions."

**Provide evidence:**  
"Tool X should replace tool Y because [breach, discontinuation, better alternative, etc. —with links to sources]."

**Respect existing structure:**  
Improvements within existing framework preferred over complete rewrites (unless proposing major version).

**One change per proposal:**  
Small, focused proposals easier to review and integrate than massive omnibus changes.

**Maintain voice:**  
New content should match existing plain-language, non-condescending, practical tone.


### Translation

**Protocol Zero should be available in multiple languages.**

**Translation priorities:**
- Maintain meaning and accessibility over literal word-for-word translation
- Adapt examples to local context where appropriate (local laws, available tools, cultural norms)
- Note where content is specific to certain jurisdictions
- Keep translated versions updated when source document changes

**Current translations:**  
English (primary)
Additional languages:  Open for community translation

**Translation maintainers:**  
Each translation should have designated maintainer responsible for keeping it current and accurate.
