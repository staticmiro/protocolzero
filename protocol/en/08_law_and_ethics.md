# 📖 PART VIII:    LAW AND ETHICS

## 33. GDPR For Humans

Laws exist to protect your data.    Most people don't know their rights or how to use them. 

GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation) is European law, but its principles have influenced data protection globally.  Many countries have similar laws.

Even if you're not in Europe, understanding these rights helps you advocate for yourself.

### Your Rights:    Access, Correction, Deletion, Portability

**Right to Access:**

You have the right to know what personal data a company holds about you. 

**How to exercise:**

Submit data access request (also called Subject Access Request or SAR).

Company must provide:  
- What data they have
- Where they got it
- Why they're processing it
- Who they share it with
- How long they'll keep it

Usually provided in digital format (downloadable file).

**Right to Correction:**

If data about you is inaccurate, you can request correction.

Examples:
- Wrong address
- Misspelled name
- Outdated information
- Incorrect categorization

Company must correct or justify why they won't. 

**Right to Deletion (Right to be Forgotten):**

You can request deletion of your personal data.

**When it applies:**
- Data no longer necessary for original purpose
- You withdraw consent (if consent was legal basis)
- Data processed unlawfully
- You object and there's no overriding legitimate reason

**When it doesn't apply:**
- Company has legal obligation to retain data (tax records, contracts)
- Data needed for legal claims
- Public interest (journalism, research)

**Right to Data Portability:**

You can request your data in structured, machine-readable format.

You can transfer this data to another service provider. 

Example:   Request all your photos and data from one social media platform, upload to competitor. 

Reduces lock-in.   Promotes competition.

**Right to Object:**

You can object to processing of your data, especially for: 
- Direct marketing (always honored)
- Profiling and automated decision-making
- Processing based on legitimate interest (company must prove compelling reason to continue)

### **[RECOMMENDED]** How to Submit a Data Deletion Request

**Process:**

**1. Find the right contact:**

Look for:
- Privacy policy (contact info usually at bottom)
- Data protection officer email
- Privacy request form on website
- General customer support (they'll route to right department)

**2. Be specific:**

Clearly state:
- Your name (as it appears in their system)
- Account identifier (email, username, customer number)
- What you're requesting (deletion of all personal data)
- Legal basis (right to deletion under applicable law)

**3. Provide identification:**

Company may ask for proof you're the account owner (prevent someone else deleting your account).

Provide minimal information necessary to verify identity.

**4. Set deadline:**

Most laws require response within 30 days.  State this in request.

**5. Keep records:**

Save copy of your request, confirmation receipt, all correspondence.

**6. Follow up:**

If no response within deadline, send reminder referencing original request and legal deadline.

**7. Escalate if necessary:**

If company refuses or ignores:  
- Request explanation of legal basis for refusal
- Contact data protection authority
- Consider legal advice for serious cases

**Template request:**

```
Subject: Data Deletion Request under [GDPR/CCPA/applicable law]

To whom it may concern,

I am requesting the deletion of all personal data you hold about me under my right to erasure as provided by [applicable data protection law].

Account details:
- Name: [Your Name]
- Email: [Your Email]
- Account ID: [If applicable]

Please confirm deletion within 30 days as required by law.

Please also inform me of any third parties with whom my data has been shared so I may make the same request. 

Regards,
[Your Name]
```

### What Companies Can and Cannot Do

**Companies CAN:**

**Collect data with legal basis:**
- You consented
- Necessary for contract (you signed up for service)
- Legal obligation (tax records)
- Legitimate interest (fraud prevention, improving service) - but you can object

**Process data for original purpose:**

You signed up for email newsletter → they can send you newsletter. 

**Keep data for reasonable time:**

Not forever, but as long as necessary for purpose.

**Share data with processors:**

Cloud hosting, payment processing, analytics - but processors must follow same rules.

**Companies CANNOT:**

**Collect data without legal basis:**

No valid reason = unlawful processing.

**Use data for purposes you didn't consent to:**

You consented to newsletter → they cannot sell your data to brokers without separate consent.

**Keep data indefinitely:**

Must delete when no longer needed.

**Transfer data outside jurisdiction without protection:**

EU data exported outside EU must have adequate protections.

**Refuse access/deletion requests without valid reason:**

Must comply or explain legal exception.

**Make service conditional on irrelevant consent:**

"Accept data sharing or you can't use the service" when data sharing isn't necessary for service = invalid consent.

### **[RECOMMENDED]** Complaints to Data Protection Authority

If company violates your rights, you can complain to regulatory authority. 

**When to complain:**

- Company ignores data requests
- Company refuses deletion without valid reason
- Data breach not properly reported
- Unlawful processing of your data
- Excessive data collection
- Data used for purposes you didn't consent to

**How to file complaint:**

**1. Identify your authority:**

Depends on jurisdiction.   Usually national data protection authority.

**2. Gather evidence:**

- Documentation of your requests to company
- Company responses (or lack thereof)
- Screenshots, emails, anything showing violation
- Timeline of events

**3. Submit complaint:**

Most authorities have online complaint forms. 

Provide: 
- Your contact information
- Company details
- Description of violation
- What you want (deletion, correction, penalty for company)
- Evidence

**4. Investigation:**

Authority reviews complaint, may contact company, may investigate.

**5. Resolution:**

Authority may: 
- Order company to comply with your request
- Fine company for violations
- Require company to change practices
- Dismiss complaint if unfounded

**Process can take months.**

**You don't need lawyer to file complaint.**  Authorities handle consumer complaints.

### Common Mistakes

**Not knowing your rights**  
Companies count on ignorance.    People don't request data because they don't know they can. 

**Accepting "that's our policy" as answer**  
Company policy doesn't override law.   If law says you have right to deletion, company must comply or cite specific legal exception.

**Giving up after first refusal**  
Escalate.   Request clarification.  Contact authority. 

**Requesting deletion then shocked service stops working**  
Deleting your data means deleting your account.  Service can't function without data.   This is expected.

**Not preserving evidence**  
Delete emails with company before filing complaint.   Makes case harder to prove.

**Accepting privacy policy without reading**  
You're consenting to data processing.   At least skim to know what you're agreeing to.

**Thinking privacy laws only protect Europeans**  
Many countries and states have adopted similar laws.  California (CCPA), Brazil (LGPD), others.   Research your jurisdiction.

### Quick Wins

- **15 minutes:** Submit data access request to one company to see what they have on you. 
- **20 minutes:** Delete account from service you no longer use, requesting full data deletion.
- **10 minutes:** Research your local data protection authority and bookmark complaint form.
- **30 minutes:** Read privacy policy of service you use most, understand what data they collect and why.


## 34. Cyberbullying and Harassment

Online harassment is real harm.   It escalates quickly.   It follows you everywhere.  

### Recognition:    Stalking, Doxxing, Revenge Porn

**Cyberbullying:**

Repeated harmful behavior online: 
- Insults, threats, humiliation
- Spreading rumors
- Exclusion from groups
- Impersonation
- Sharing embarrassing content

**Cyberstalking:**

Persistent unwanted contact online:
- Monitoring your social media obsessively
- Sending repeated messages after you've asked them to stop
- Creating fake accounts to contact you
- Tracking your location
- Showing up where you are based on online posts

**Doxxing:**

Publishing private information with intent to harm:
- Home address
- Phone number
- Workplace
- Family members' information
- Financial information
- Medical records

Goal is usually to intimidate or invite others to harass you.

**Revenge Porn (Non-consensual Intimate Images):**

Sharing intimate photos or videos without consent:
- After relationship ends
- As blackmail
- For humiliation
- For profit

**All of these are serious.    Many are crimes.**

### **[CRITICAL]** How to Document

If you're being harassed, document everything.  

**Why documentation matters:**

- Proves pattern of behavior
- Essential for legal action
- Shows escalation
- Supports protective orders
- Demonstrates impact

**What to document:**

**Screenshots:**
- Harassing messages
- Threatening posts
- Doxxing (where your information was published)
- Profile pages of harasser
- Comments, tags, shares

**Include in screenshot:**
- Full message/post
- Date and time
- Username of sender
- URL (if web-based)
- Context (previous messages if relevant)

**Metadata:**
- Save original files when possible (screenshots can be challenged as fake; original files have metadata proving authenticity)
- Email headers (show sender information)
- Message timestamps

**Written log:**
- Date and time of each incident
- What happened
- Who was involved
- How it made you feel (demonstrates impact)
- Any witnesses

**Physical evidence:**
- Unwanted gifts, letters
- Evidence of in-person stalking following online harassment

**Don't delete harassing messages** (even though you want to).  You need them as evidence.

**Back up documentation** in multiple locations.  Cloud storage, external drive, printed copies.

### **[RECOMMENDED]** Reporting to Platforms, Police, Organizations

**Report to platform:**

Social media, messaging apps, forums have reporting mechanisms.

**Process:**
1. Find report/flag option (usually on post or message)
2. Select reason (harassment, threats, doxxing, intimate images)
3. Provide context
4. Include evidence (screenshots if form allows)

**Platforms may:**
- Remove content
- Suspend or ban harasser's account
- Provide you with information for legal action (in response to court order)

**Platform response is often slow or inadequate.**  Report anyway.  Creates record.

**Report to police:**

Depending on severity and local laws:

**When to report:**
- Credible threats of violence
- Stalking (online and offline)
- Doxxing that results in real-world harassment
- Revenge porn
- Harassment of minors
- Extortion or blackmail

**How to report:**
1. Contact local police (in-person often better than phone)
2. Bring documentation (printed screenshots, written log)
3. Clearly explain what happened, how long it's been going on, why you fear for safety
4. Request case number
5. Ask about protective/restraining orders
6. Follow up if no action taken

**Police understanding of cyber crimes varies.**  Some jurisdictions have specialized units.  Others are learning.

Be persistent.    This is real crime with real harm. 

**Report to support organizations:**

Non-profits exist to help harassment victims: 

Services offered:
- Emotional support
- Safety planning
- Legal referrals
- Technical assistance (securing accounts, removing content)
- Advocacy

**Find organizations specializing in:**
- Domestic violence (for intimate partner harassment)
- Stalking victims
- Revenge porn/non-consensual imagery
- Cyberbullying (especially for minors)
- Hate crimes (if harassment is based on identity)

Online search for "[your location] cyber harassment help" or check national organizations' websites.

### **[CRITICAL]** Account Protection After Attack

If you're being harassed, secure your accounts. 

**Immediate actions:**

**1. Change all passwords:**
- Use password manager
- Unique, strong passwords for each account
- Prioritize email (controls password resets for other accounts)

**2. Enable 2FA everywhere:**
- Authenticator app, not SMS (harasser might have convinced phone company to transfer your number)

**3. Review account security:**
- Check active sessions, log out unrecognized devices
- Check recovery email and phone number (harasser sometimes changes these to take over account)
- Review connected apps, revoke suspicious ones
- Check account activity logs for unauthorized access

**4. Lock down privacy settings:**
- Make profiles private/friends-only
- Disable tagging without approval
- Hide friends/followers list
- Remove location from posts
- Disable "find me by phone/email" features

**5. Create separation:**
- Don't engage with harasser
- Block on all platforms
- Filter messages from non-contacts to separate folder
- Consider new accounts under pseudonyms for friends/family

**6. Secure personal information:**
- Remove address, phone, workplace from all public profiles
- Google yourself, request removal from people-search sites
- Lock down social media of family members (they're targets too)
- Inform workplace/school security of situation

**7. Document security measures:**
- Save confirmation emails
- Note dates when you made changes
- Demonstrates you took reasonable precautions

### Common Mistakes

**Engaging with harasser**  
Responding (even to tell them to stop) often escalates.  Block and document.

**Deleting evidence because it's painful**  
Understandable, but makes legal action harder.  Archive, then hide from view.

**Assuming platform will handle it**  
Platforms are inconsistent.   Don't rely solely on platform action.

**Not taking threats seriously**  
"It's just online" dismissal is dangerous.  Online threats escalate to offline harm.

**Trying to handle alone**  
Shame, embarrassment, fear prevent people from seeking help.  You don't deserve this.  Get support.

**Public confrontation**  
Calling out harasser publicly often invites pile-on from their followers.  Handle through official channels.

**Not protecting family and friends**  
Harassers target people close to you.  Warn them, help them secure accounts.

**Giving up after first police report dismissed**  
Escalate.   Request supervisor.   Contact specialized cyber crime units.  File multiple reports as harassment continues.

### Quick Wins (If Being Harassed)

- **30 minutes:** Screenshot and save all current harassment evidence to secure location. 
- **20 minutes:** Change passwords on all accounts, enable 2FA. 
- **15 minutes:** Lock down privacy settings on all social media. 
- **Ongoing:** Block harasser on every platform, do not engage. 

**If you're supporting someone being harassed:**

- Believe them
- Don't minimize ("just ignore it")
- Offer to help with documentation
- Research reporting options together
- Provide emotional support
- Respect their choices about how to handle it


## 35. Digital Responsibility

You have power online.   Power to share, comment, create, influence. 

With power comes responsibility. 

### What You Can Be Liable For

**Defamation:**

Publishing false statements that harm someone's reputation.

Written:  libel.   Spoken: slander.  Online: usually libel.

**Not protected by free speech if:**
- Statement is false
- Presented as fact (not opinion)
- Causes harm
- Made with negligence or malice

**You can be sued** for defamatory posts, comments, reviews. 

**Copyright infringement:**

Using someone else's creative work without permission: 
- Photos, videos, music, text, art
- Posting, sharing, downloading, modifying

**"I found it on the internet" is not defense.**

**Fair use is limited** (commentary, criticism, education, parody - with restrictions).

**If you didn't create it and don't have permission, don't post it.**

**Harassment and threats:**

Online harassment is illegal in many jurisdictions. 

Threats of violence (even "just joking") can result in criminal charges.

**Sharing illegal content:**

Child sexual abuse material (CSAM), non-consensual intimate images, stolen data, pirated software. 

Distributing is crime.   Possessing is often crime.

**Identity theft and fraud:**

Impersonating someone online, creating fake accounts, phishing, scams.

**Incitement:**

Encouraging illegal activity, violence, harm.

**You are responsible for what you post, share, and comment.**

"I was just sharing" or "I didn't create it" is not always defense.  Distribution can be as liable as creation.

### **[RECOMMENDED]** Copyright:    What You Can and Cannot Copy

**You CAN:**

**Use your own original work:**  
You created it, you own copyright (unless you signed it away via contract).

**Use public domain work:**  
Copyright expired, or creator released it to public domain.

**Use with permission:**  
Copyright holder gave you explicit permission (license, contract, verbal agreement - get it in writing).

**Fair use (limited and context-dependent):**  
- Commentary and criticism (quoting small portions to discuss/review)
- Education (teaching, research - with restrictions)
- Parody (making fun of original work)
- News reporting (quoting relevant portions)

Fair use is decided case-by-case.  Not blanket permission.  

**Use Creative Commons licensed work:**  
Creator granted specific permissions (attribution, non-commercial, no derivatives, etc.).  Follow license terms.

**You CANNOT:**

**Copy and post someone else's photo/video/text:**  
Even if you credit them.   Even if you link to source.  Even if it's on public website.

Copyright holder decides how work is distributed. 

**Download and re-upload copyrighted content:**  
Movies, music, books, software without permission.

**Modify and post someone else's work:**  
Adding filter to photo doesn't make it yours.  Editing video doesn't transfer copyright.

**Use for commercial purposes without license:**  
Even if non-commercial use might be fair, commercial use requires permission.

**"Everyone else does it" is not legal defense.**

### Child Safety Online Basics

**Recognizing illegal content:**

CSAM (Child Sexual Abuse Material) is any sexual image or video of minor. 

Includes:
- Real photos/videos
- Computer-generated imagery
- Cartoons/drawings in some jurisdictions

**If you encounter it:**

**Do not:**
- Save it
- Share it (even to report to friends/platform)
- Investigate further
- Try to identify child or location

**Do:**
- Report immediately to platform
- Report to authorities (national cyber tip lines exist in most countries)
- Clear cache/history
- If downloaded accidentally, do not delete (tampering with evidence) - report first, follow authority instructions

**Reporting does not get you in trouble.**  Authorities distinguish between accidental exposure and intentional possession.

**Protecting minors you're responsible for:**

- Age-appropriate internet access and supervision
- Education about not sharing personal information
- Monitoring (balanced with age and privacy)
- Open communication (they should feel safe telling you if something bad happens online)
- Reporting if they encounter abuse or exploitation

**This is serious crime.**  Always report.   You might save a child. 

### Ethics of Sharing:    Deepfakes, Disinformation

**Deepfakes:**

AI-generated fake videos/images that appear real.

Uses:
- Political disinformation (fake speeches, actions)
- Non-consensual pornography (face-swapping)
- Fraud (impersonating someone for scams)
- Defamation

**Creating and sharing deepfakes:**

Many jurisdictions making this illegal, especially: 
- Non-consensual sexual imagery
- Election interference
- Fraud

Even where legal, it's unethical.   Causes real harm.

**If you see content that seems fake:**  
- Don't share it
- Check original sources
- Reverse image search
- Look for verification from credible outlets

**Disinformation:**

False information deliberately spread to deceive.

**Before sharing news/information:**

Ask: 
- **Is this from credible source?**  Random blog vs established news outlet. 
- **Can I verify this elsewhere?**  Multiple independent sources confirm? 
- **Does headline match content?**  Clickbait headlines often misrepresent article. 
- **Am I being manipulated emotionally?**  Outrage, fear, excitement clouds judgment. 
- **What's the date? **  Old story recirculated as if current. 
- **Is image/video authentic?**  Reverse image search, check for editing.

**If unsure, don't share.**

**Harm from disinformation:**
- Election manipulation
- Public health crises (vaccine/medical misinformation)
- Violence (conspiracy theories leading to real-world attacks)
- Destroyed reputations
- Erosion of trust in institutions

**You have responsibility not to amplify lies.**

### Common Mistakes

**"It's just a joke" defense**  
Doesn't protect from legal liability or harm caused. 

**Sharing without verifying**  
"I saw it online" spreads misinformation.   Verify before sharing.

**Assuming deletion removes liability**  
Post is captured in screenshots, archives.   Deleted doesn't mean gone.

**Not reading before sharing**  
Headline is outrageous.  Share immediately.   Article says opposite of headline.  Too late.

**Piling on in harassment campaigns**  
"Everyone is criticizing this person, I'll add my voice."   You're participating in harassment.

**Sharing personal info in outrage**  
"This person did bad thing, here's their address!"   That's doxxing.  Illegal.  Don't. 

**Copyright ignorance**  
"I didn't know" doesn't prevent liability.   Educate yourself.

**Thinking anonymity absolves responsibility**  
Pseudonymous account doesn't make you untraceable.  You're still liable for what you post.

### Quick Wins

- **15 minutes:** Review your recent posts/shares for anything that might be copyright infringement, defamation, or misinformation.  Delete if questionable.
- **10 minutes:** Learn how to report illegal content on platforms you use most.
- **20 minutes:** Educate yourself on copyright basics and fair use in your jurisdiction.
- **Ongoing:** Before sharing anything, pause and ask:   "Is this true?  Could this harm someone?  Do I have the right to share this?"

*Digital citizenship is real citizenship.  Your actions online have real consequences.  Act with integrity.*
